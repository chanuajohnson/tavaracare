# Tavara Writing Style & Anti-AI Guardrails

Voice: warm, plainspoken, Trinbagonian-rooted. Founder talking to a family member, not a brand talking to a market.

## Core principles
1. **Clarity over cleverness.** If a sentence needs to be re-read, cut it.
2. **Specific over abstract.** "Bathing at 6am after a stroke" beats "comprehensive care solutions."
3. **Earned, not announced.** Show competence; never claim "trusted leader," "world-class," "premier."
4. **Caribbean cadence is welcome.** Local nouns (mauby, neighbour, church member, Diego Martin) ground the writing. Don't sanitise.
5. **Emotional honesty.** Name the hard thing (guilt, shame, exhaustion) before offering the fix.

## DO
- Short paragraphs (1–4 sentences). Mobile-first.
- Concrete nouns: "a Certificate of Character", "an 8am shift", "$45/hr".
- Direct address: "you", "your mother", "your family".
- British spellings (organise, realise, neighbour, behaviour) — T&T standard.
- One idea per sentence. Break the long ones.
- Repetition for rhythm is fine when intentional. Repetition because the AI lost the plot is not.

## DON'T — hard bans
- **No em-dashes (—) or en-dashes (–).** Use comma, full stop, colon, or rewrite. This is the single clearest AI tell.
- **No "It's not just X — it's Y" construction.** Or any variant ("more than just", "not merely").
- **No "In today's fast-paced world / landscape / realm / journey / tapestry."**
- **No "delve, leverage, navigate, foster, unlock, unleash, empower, elevate, holistic, nuanced, multifaceted, robust, seamless, cutting-edge, game-changer, ever-evolving, transformative."**
- **No "Moreover / Furthermore / In conclusion / It's worth noting / It's important to note."**
- **No tricolons of abstract nouns** ("strength, courage, and resilience"). If you list three things, make them concrete and specific.
- **No "Whether you're X, Y, or Z..." opener.** Cliché.
- **No rhetorical question stacks** ("What does this mean? Why does it matter? How do we fix it?"). One question, then answer it.
- **No "Imagine this:" / "Picture this:" openers.**
- **No bolded summary sentence at the end of every section.** Trust the reader.
- **No emoji in long-form blog body.** (Product UI is fine; editorial copy is not.)
- **No Oxford-comma lists of vague virtues** ("dignity, respect, and compassion") without a concrete anchor.

## Punctuation rules
- Em-dash → comma, period, or colon depending on intent.
  - Aside / pause → comma.
  - New independent thought → full stop.
  - Introducing a list or explanation → colon.
- En-dash in ranges ("8 AM – 4 PM") → "8am to 4pm".
- Ellipses sparingly. Never to sound thoughtful.
- One exclamation mark per article maximum. Usually zero.

## Structural smell-tests (reject the draft if any are true)
- Every section ends with a one-line moral.
- Lists are exactly 3 or 5 items every time.
- The piece never names a person, place, dollar amount, or hour.
- The closing paragraph starts with "Ultimately," "At the end of the day," or "In the end."
- The intro re-states the headline in different words instead of opening on a scene.
- Sentences average more than 22 words.

## Tavara-specific vocabulary
- "Care coordination" (not "agency", "service provider", "platform partner").
- "Caregiver" or "care professional" (not "worker", "staff", "employee").
- "Family" (not "client", "customer").
- "Match" / "placement" for hiring, "shift" for working hours, "rotation" for team coverage.
- Always: **tavara.care** (lowercase, no www).

## Editing pass checklist (run before publishing)
1. Search the doc for `—` and `–`. Must return zero.
2. Search for the banned word list above. Must return zero.
3. Read the first sentence aloud. Does it sound like a person or a press release?
4. Find the longest sentence. Cut it in half.
5. Find the most abstract paragraph. Replace one noun with a real-world example.
6. Confirm at least one local detail (T&T place, currency, custom) per 800 words.

## Prompt block for AI-assisted drafts
Paste this verbatim when asking any AI to draft or edit Tavara copy:

Hard rules: no em-dashes or en-dashes ever (use commas, periods, colons); no "It's not just X, it's Y"; no words like delve, leverage, navigate, foster, holistic, robust, seamless, journey, landscape, realm, tapestry, transformative, unlock, empower, elevate; no "Moreover/Furthermore/In conclusion"; no emoji in body copy; no rhetorical question stacks; no bolded section morals. British spellings. Short paragraphs. Concrete nouns and real T&T details (places, dollar amounts, hours). One idea per sentence. If a sentence sounds like marketing, rewrite it.
